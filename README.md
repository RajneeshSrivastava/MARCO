## Staphylococcus aureus Biofilm Infection Impairs Efferocytosis in Wound-Macrophages via Induction of a MARCO-high MerTK-low Subset
#### Authors: 
Piya Das Ghatak, Fabio Muniz de Oliveira, Anita Yadav, Ahmed Safwat Abouhashem, Rajneesh Srivastava, Pradipta Banerjee, Shayani Mukherjee, Surabhi Singh, Kanhaiya Singh, Cecilia Zamarripa, Gayle M. Gordillo, Chandan K. Sen, Subhadip Ghatak, Sashwati Roy*

McGowan Institute for Regenerative Medicine, Department of Surgery, University of Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, 15219, USA
University of Pittsburgh Medical Center (UPMC), Pittsburgh, PA, United States
* Correspondence should be addressed to: Sashwati Roy, PhD 	(E-mail: sar453@pitt.edu)



#### Abstract
Biofilm-forming Staphylococcus aureus infections impair macrophage (mφ)-mediated immune functions disrupting inflammation resolution  and tissue repair for chronic wound healing. In this work, we demonstrate that S. aureus biofilm elicits a spatially defined MARCOhiMERTKlo mφ niche that exhibits compromised efferocytosis. Isogenic S. aureus USA300 mutants with low (ΔsarA) or high (ΔrexB) biofilm-forming abilities, demonstrated that only high biofilm conditioned media (CM) fsignificantly impaired efferocytosis in human monocyte-derived mφ (MDM). Neutralizing MARCO in MDM restored efferocytosis, implicating MARCO as a negative regulator of efferocytosis . Mechanistically, ΔrexB-CM induced and facilitated the nuclear translocation of CEBP/β, suppressing MERTK expression. In a murine excisional wound model, myeloid-specific overexpression of MARCO reduced the clearance of apoptotic cells with persistence inflammation in the granulation tissue. Collectively, this work provides mechanistic insight into how S. aureus biofilms subvert host immune cells and identifed MARCOhiMERTKlo subset as a controllable immunomodulatory target for therapeutic intervention in biofilm-associated tissue pathologies.
